01 — Tracks

Pick your lane.
Or do both.

Two courses run side by side, each taught by the officers who actually do that work. One membership covers everything.

Research

Aarav Sinha, Sharvin Goyal

Build models. Publish papers.

You learn how models actually work, train your own, run real experiments, and write the results up for publication — the same pipeline your instructors use at ICML and NeurIPS workshops.

  • Neural networks, reinforcement learning, and LLMs from the ground up
  • Design and run your own experiments, not just tutorials
  • Write the paper: methods, results, figures, the whole thing
  • Submit to high school research symposiums and workshop tracks
Build

Robert Pierson

Vibecode. CAD. Ship it.

Use AI as a tool to make real things fast — full apps, designed parts, working products — and learn enough to stay the engineer instead of the passenger when the AI gets it wrong.

  • Vibecoding with AI agents: ship full apps, then learn to review the output
  • AI-assisted CAD: prompt or sketch to a real, printable, manufacturable part
  • Websites, bots, tools, hardware — portfolio pieces you own
  • Deployment, version control, and how to not lose your work

04 — First six weeks

What actually
happens.

No mystery about what you're signing up for. This is the plan through early October — every date is a Wednesday, 2:45–3:15 PM, Room 1661.

  1. Sept 2

    Kickoff

    Free pizza and candy. What the club does, who runs it, and how the two tracks work. Pick the one you want. Nothing due, nothing to pay.

  2. Sept 9

    Get set up

    Python, Google Colab, and a GitHub account. Everyone trains and runs their first model before they leave the room.

  3. Sept 16

    Tracks split

    Research: how a neural network actually learns, gradient by gradient. Build: your first vibecoded app, shipped live.

  4. Sept 23

    Pick your project

    Research: choose the question you'll spend the semester on. Build: AI-assisted CAD, prompt to printable part.

  5. Sept 30

    Work session

    Heads-down building with officers on hand. Competition sign-ups open — this is when the shirt starts mattering.

  6. Oct 7

    Show what you made

    Everyone demos. Five minutes, no slides required. First look at what the club submits this year.

Four steps.

The interest form is the only online part. Everything else happens in person at a meeting.

  1. Fill out the interest form

    One form for everyone — members and anyone who wants to be an officer. Open the form. Takes about a minute.

  2. Get the permission slip signed

    Katy ISD requires a signed parent permission slip for every non-curriculum club. Download it here, or find it on the OTHS clubs page.

  3. Bring it to a Wednesday meeting

    Room 1661, 2:45–3:15 PM. Hand the signed slip to Mrs. Dennison. Forms have to be turned in fresh each school year.

    $20 for general membership, or $30 for membership plus the shirt. Pay at the same meeting you turn in your slip and you're in.

10 — Questions

Before you ask.

If it's not here, come to a meeting or message the club on Instagram.

Do I need any experience?

No. We start people from zero every year — that's the whole point of the first few weeks. If you've never written a line of Python, you're the person this club is built for.

Do I need my own laptop?

Bring one if you have one, since everything runs in the browser through Google Colab. If you don't have a laptop, tell an officer at the first meeting and we'll sort something out. It isn't a reason to skip joining.

What if I can't afford the dues?

Talk to Mrs. Dennison or any officer privately. Nobody gets turned away over $20 — we'll figure it out and we won't make it a thing.

Can I join after September?

Yes. Show up at any Wednesday meeting with the signed permission slip and your dues. You'll have some catching up to do on the track content, but officers will get you current.

Do I have to pick one track and stick with it?

No. One membership covers both. Plenty of people do research one week and build the next — the tracks run in the same room at the same time.

Is the shirt actually required?

Only if you compete. For competitions it's mandatory, no exceptions. If you think you'll compete at any point this year, the $30 combo saves you $5 over buying both separately later.

Where do I get updates between meetings?

Two places. The Discord is the day-to-day — project help, competition news, general chatter. Remind (@othsml) is for announcements you actually need to see, like a room change. Join both; they do different jobs.

How do I become an officer?

Same interest form as everyone else — there's a question on it. You don't need a research background. Officers get picked on showing up and following through, which is rarer than it sounds.

Is this actually worth it for college apps?

A published paper or a shipped project with your name on it is worth more than a membership line on a list. That's why the club is built around producing those instead of just meeting.
